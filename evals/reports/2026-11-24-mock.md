<details><summary>Plan under review</summary>

**Learning objective**

> By the end of the period, students can move a term across the equals sign by changing its sign (+ becomes −, × becomes ÷), and check the answer by substitution.

**Prerequisite check** (3 min)

> Two quick questions, answered on fingers or in rough work. If many get Q1 wrong, spend one minute on adding and subtracting negative numbers first.

**Hook** (4 min)

> Draw a balance (taraazu) on the board with 3x + 5 on one pan and 20 on the other. Ask: "If I take 5 away from the left pan, what must I do to the right pan to keep it level?" Take two or three answers before moving on.

**Explanation — #1** (8 min)

> 21 students moved a term across "=" without changing its sign — for example "3x = 20 + 5" (Periodic Test 1, Q7). Show why it is wrong: subtract 5 from BOTH sides — 3x + 5 − 5 = 20 − 5 — so 3x = 20 − 5. "Transposing" is only a shortcut for doing the same thing to both sides; the sign flips because we subtracted. Write the rule: + ↔ −, × ↔ ÷ when a term crosses "=".

**Worked example** (7 min)

> Solve 4x + 5 = 2x + 13 together. Step 1: 4x − 2x = 13 − 5. Step 2: 2x = 8. Step 3: x = 4. Check: 4(4) + 5 = 21, 2(4) + 13 = 21 ✓. Ask a student to say each sign change aloud as you write it.

**Tackle a misconception — #2** (6 min)

> 8 students applied an operation to one side only, e.g. "3x + 5 − 5 = 20 → 3x = 20" (Periodic Test 1, Q7). Put it on the board without saying whose it is and ask: "Is the balance still level?" Then fix it together: whatever you do to the left, do to the right. (4 more had the right method but slipped on arithmetic — remind everyone to check by substituting.)

**Guided practice** (7 min)

> In the peer pairs below, solve: (a) x + 9 = 4, (b) 3y − 7 = 11, (c) 12 − 2p = 4. The mentor explains each sign change; the learner writes. Walk round and check line 1 of every answer — that is where the sign error shows up.

**Exit ticket** (5 min)

> Students attempt the 4 exit-ticket questions alone on a half sheet with name and roll number. Collect them, photograph them and upload them to RedPen for grading.
